# AI Language Evaluation Methodology

## 1. Project Purpose
This independent project investigates how AI language models interpret Kurdish expressions, regional vocabulary, and Turkish-Kurdish code-switching in everyday communication, with particular interest in Central Anatolia.

The goal is to identify comprehension errors, document human feedback, and support more accurate and context-sensitive multilingual AI evaluation.

## 2. Evaluation Procedure
Each test follows these steps:

1. Prepare an original or permission-cleared language sample.
2. Submit the sample to an AI model without providing its intended meaning.
3. Record the model's initial interpretation without modifying it.
4. Obtain a human evaluation of the intended meaning.
5. Compare the model's response with the human-provided interpretation.
6. Classify confirmed errors and record any unresolved ambiguity.
7. Document the findings and limitations.

## 3. Evaluation Criteria

### Semantic Accuracy
Does the model preserve the intended meaning of the expression?

### Information Completeness
Does the interpretation include all important details without omissions?

### Regional Vocabulary
Does the model correctly interpret local expressions and culturally specific terminology?

### Contextual Understanding
Does the model account for the meaning of words within the surrounding conversation?

### Uncertainty Handling
Does the model acknowledge ambiguous or unfamiliar expressions rather than inventing unsupported meanings?

### Code-Switching
When Turkish and Kurdish are both present, does the model correctly interpret transitions between the languages?

## 4. Error Categories
- SEM: Incorrect interpretation of meaning.
- OMIT: Missing information.
- REG: Misinterpretation of regional vocabulary.
- CONTEXT: Failure to understand contextual meaning.
- UNSUPPORTED: Interpretation presented without sufficient evidence.
- CS: Error associated with language switching.

A single response may contain more than one error category.

## 5. Result Classification
- Correct: The essential meaning is accurately preserved.
- Partially Correct: Some meaning is preserved, but important errors or omissions remain.
- Incorrect: The essential meaning is substantially misunderstood.
- Unresolved: Available evidence is insufficient to determine accuracy.

## 6. Human Verification
Initial evaluations are provided by one human contributor familiar with the expressions being tested.

These evaluations are documented as human judgments, not independent linguistic certification. Disagreements and uncertain word meanings should be recorded rather than silently resolved.

## 7. Research Limitations
- The project currently contains a small number of exploratory examples.
- Testing has not been independently replicated.
- Regional vocabulary may vary between speakers and communities.
- The examples should not be treated as representative of all Kurdish varieties.
- Model-reported confidence is subjective and not statistically calibrated.
- Not every Kurdish sentence constitutes Turkish-Kurdish code-switching.

## 8. Data Ethics
Only original, publicly usable, or permission-cleared language examples should be published.

Private conversations, identifying details, copyrighted project materials, and confidential recordings must not be shared without appropriate authorization.

## 9. Project Status
Exploratory evaluation in progress. Findings are preliminary and are not evidence of general model performance.
